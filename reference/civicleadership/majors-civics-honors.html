Overview

Thoughtful Leadership
Starts Here

The Bachelor of Arts in Civics Honors connects students with deep study of the American experience and the intellectual origins of ordered liberty.

The major introduces students to essential questions of constitutional self-government in the Intellectual Foundations sequence, cultivates deep skills in the Tools Sequence, and equips students with studies in Constitutionalism, Western Civilization, and Civic Leadership.

With emphasis on interdisciplinary study and career discernment, complete with thesis and internship, students gain experience for a life of service.

Course Highlights

Civics Honors majors read texts  as well as  trends to combine political judgment with empirical insight.

The Intellectual Foundations sequence introduces students to the key questions of self-government. Tools courses give students practices in the skills of leadership as they learn to read, write, think, speak, deliberate, and persuade.

Students gain broad understanding through three areas of study:

Constitutionalism

Courses in Constitutionalism offer sustained engagement with the central ideas and tensions of American constitutional self-government.

Western Civilization

The major situates the American experiment in its intellectual context with courses that explore Western Civilization through philosophical, theological, and political sources from antiquity to today.

Civic Leadership

Courses in Civic Leadership integrate classical rhetoric, ethics, statesmanship, and public affairs with serious training in economics, data analysis, and markets in order to equip students with tools for republican self-government.

Get in Touch With Us

Why do all Civics Honors majors complete an internship?

The Civics Honors major prepares students to lead through a distinctive combination of classical and professional education. From First Year Foundations through graduation, Civics Honors majors prepare for purposeful work. The internship is a key step in that journey. Learn more about the internship here.

Why does every Civics Honors major write a thesis?

Every new technology we make reveals our need for wisdom about what it means to be human and to live well together. In a moment of algorithm-driven text, it’s more important than ever to be able to articulate a compelling question that drives thoughtful analysis. The requirement for a year-long, mentored thesis enables Civics Honors majors to cultivate deep skills of thinking, scholarship, and compelling communication.

Is there a Foreign Language requirement in Civics Honors?

Yes. Civics Honors students must meet the Foreign Language Intermediate Proficiency requirement. This can be fulfilled in several ways: by taking a placement exam at UT Austin to test out, completing the required coursework at UT Austin, or transferring in approved coursework from another institution. Meet with your SCL Advisor, who can guide you through this process.

As an admitted Civics Honors student, am I allowed to take courses in the new SCL major?

Yes. The Great Books (GB) major will officially launch in Fall 2027. Beginning in Fall 2026, GB courses will be offered, and all UT students may enroll in available courses.
